# What a policy or procedure is

This page explains Steward's two document types and how they are grouped.

A **policy** is a written rule: it tells people in your organisation how to do something the
right way. A **procedure** is a written set of steps: it walks through how to actually carry a
policy out. Both are first-class documents in Steward — they share the same categories, the same
lifecycle, and the same acknowledgement model, so everything else in this guide applies equally
to both unless a page says otherwise.

Steward is the one place where all your policies and procedures live. Instead of hunting through
email or shared drives, you come here to read the one that matters to you.

## 🗂️ Categories

Every policy and procedure lives in a **category**. A category is a topic that holds related
documents together, like "Safety" or "Human Resources." Categories make documents easy to find
and easy to manage.

Categories are arranged in a **tree**: a category can have child categories under it, and those
children can have their own children. A broad topic sits near the top, and more specific topics
branch off below it. For example, a top-level "Human Resources" category might hold a "Leave"
child, which in turn holds "Parental Leave."

## 🔽 Settings flow down the tree

Categories carry **settings** that shape how the documents inside them behave: which template a
new draft starts from, who can read and approve a document, and how acknowledgement works.

These settings **inherit** down the tree. A child category takes its parent's settings unless
someone sets a different value on the child. When a child sets its own value, that value wins for
the child and everything under it — the closest, most specific setting is the one that takes
effect.

:::note
Inheritance saves work. Set a value once on a parent category, and every category below it
follows the same rule until you decide to change one.
:::
