# Acknowledgement

This page explains what it means to acknowledge a policy or procedure, who has to do it, and
when.

To **acknowledge** a document means to confirm that you have read it. When you acknowledge, Steward
records that you have seen it, along with the date — like signing your name to say "Yes, I read
this."

## ✅ Why acknowledgement matters

Some documents are important enough that your organisation needs proof that people have read
them. Acknowledgement gives that proof: for you, a clear record that you did your part; for your
organisation, evidence the right people know the rules, often needed for safety, training and
legal reasons.

## 👥 Who must acknowledge

Not every document needs acknowledgement, and not everyone has to acknowledge the ones that do.
Whether you can acknowledge a document — and whether you are required to — is controlled by the
category's access rules; see [Who can do what](/concepts/access-model).

## ⏰ When to acknowledge: the trigger

The access rules say *who*. The **trigger** says *when* an acknowledgement is required.

- **On publish** — Users acknowledge once, when the document is published. A later new version
  does **not** force people to acknowledge again.
- **On change** — Users must acknowledge the current version each time it changes.
- **No trigger** — No acknowledgement is required at all.

## 🔄 Who must acknowledge is always live

Steward works out who owes an acknowledgement **live**, from today's access rules and today's
group membership. Nothing is frozen in place.

- Change a category's access rules, and who must acknowledge updates right away.
- Add or remove someone from a directory group referenced in the rules, and their obligations
  update too.
- While a user still has Acknowledge access to a document's category, their acknowledgement
  stands — rule changes that still include them do not ask them to sign again.
- If a user **loses** Acknowledge access, their acknowledgement for that document is **cleared**.
  If they later gain access again, they acknowledge fresh.

:::warning
Acknowledging a document means you have read and understood it. Take a moment to read the whole
thing before you confirm.
:::
