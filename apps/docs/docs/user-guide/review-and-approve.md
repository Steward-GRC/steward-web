# Review and approve

This page is for **approvers** — the people a workflow sends a draft to for a decision. It shows
how to review a submitted document and either approve it or ask for changes.

Who can approve depends on the document's category. Approvers are either owners of that category,
or users (or groups) the category's access rules grant Approve access to.

## 👀 Find what is waiting for you

When an author submits a draft, it enters an approval workflow and moves to the stage you are on.
Your **Approvals** page groups what involves you:

- **Pending** — yours to decide now. Each row shows the document and its number, the stage and
  where it sits ("Final approval · 2 of 2"), and when it's due. A red **Overdue** pill marks one
  past its due date. Select **Review** to open it.
- **Coming to you** — you're an approver on a later stage. It's marked **Upcoming**, with where
  the run is now; select **Preview** to read it ahead of your turn. You're notified when it
  reaches you.
- **In progress** — runs you've already decided that are still moving through their remaining
  stages.

A group with nothing in it is left out. When nothing is pending, the page says "No pending
approvals". On a phone, each row becomes a card.

## 👀 Review a draft

1. **Open the item.** The page names the document, its number and version, "in review · stage N
   of M", and when your stage is due. The stepper below shows every stage: who has approved (with
   their comment), who is still pending, and which stage is deciding now. A workflow with more
   than four stages shows them as a list you can open one at a time.
2. **Read the changes.** The **Changes vs the current published version** tab marks what is
   different, word by word: added words are green and underlined, removed words are red and
   struck through, and sections that didn't change fold into one line. **Side by side** shows
   the old and new text next to each other. For a first version there's nothing to compare yet.
   **Upcoming version (under review)** shows the whole draft as it will read, and **History**
   its record so far.
3. **Decide.** Write a **Decision comment** first; **Approve** and **Reject** stay off until you
   do, because the comment is recorded with your decision.
   - **Approve** — the draft passes your stage.
   - **Reject** — the draft is sent back to the author instead of moving on.

   Once you decide, the page confirms it in place of the decision panel.

If it isn't your turn, the page says why instead of offering a decision: a **Preview** of a stage
that comes to you later, **Not in your approval scope** when you can read the draft but aren't
an approver on it, or **No longer in review** when there's nothing left to decide.

## ✅ What your decision does

A workflow has ordered stages, and each stage needs a **quorum** — one approver, a majority, or
all of them — before it passes.

- When a stage still needs more approvals, your approval moves it toward its quorum.
- When you complete the **last** stage's quorum, the document is **published** automatically.
- If you reject it, the draft goes back to the author to revise and resubmit.
